NeMO mobility operator · numerical verification

Is the grand mobility still symmetric once the treecode handles the far field?

No. But the near field is, the cause is provable, and the asymmetry turns out to be the treecode's own truncation error rather than a defect on top of it. A larger problem showed up alongside it.

What to do. The symmetry defect is real but second-order. The translation-only far field is 6× larger, systematic, and makes particle rotation rates untrustworthy in any treecode run — fix that first. For symmetry itself, dropping θ from 0.3 to 0.2 costs 36 % step time and buys 3.4× on both asymmetry and far-field accuracy; a symmetrized apply is exact and costs about the same.

01

The near field is symmetric

Mob_Nbody_Torch with far_field_2b=None — self plus two-body NN plus n-body NN, nothing else — assembled column by column into a 900×900 matrix gives ‖M−Mᵀ‖/‖M‖ = 4.85×10⁻⁹ and max|M−Mᵀ| = 1.40×10⁻⁹. Symmetric to float32 precision.

Worth recording because src/model_archs.py warns that the two-body kernel M_t is deliberately not symmetric. That turns out to be exactly the condition that makes the assembled matrix symmetric — with RT = c(r)·L3(d̂) and L3(−d̂) = −L3(d̂) = L3(d̂)ᵀ, the directed-edge scatter in PairVelKernel lands the transpose in the right place on its own.

02

The far field is not — and the multipole acceptance is provably the cause

The tree is the cause. Below θ ≈ 0.15 no node passes the acceptance test, traversal degenerates to all-direct pairs, and the result is bitwise symmetric: rpy_far_velocity_pair3x3 builds a tensor that is symmetric and even in r, and with a unit force on a single particle there is no summation to reorder. Asymmetry switches on exactly when the tree starts lumping sources.

The near/far partition is clean. At θ = 0 the assembled block matches the dense reference to 8×10⁻⁸. The closest_dist_sq < 36.0 guard in warp/native/bvh.h:640 and the dot(rvec,rvec) < near_cutoff2 skip in treecode.py:178 really do produce the exact r ≥ 6 partition — no double counting, no gaps.

The asymmetry is the truncation error. The ratio between them sits at √2 = 1.414 at onset and drifts only to about 1.33. If the treecode error E = M − M_exact were uncorrelated with its own transpose you would get exactly √2; landing at or just under it means essentially none of E is a symmetric common mode. There is no separate asymmetry problem to solve — shrink the truncation error and the asymmetry shrinks one-for-one.

03

Treecode on versus off

Full 900×900 grand mobility at N = 150, θ = 0.3, assembled both ways:

Two conclusions. The treecode is the sole source of asymmetry. And the treecode is not the source of the negative eigenvalues: both operators have exactly 33, with near-identical minima. The grand mobility is already non-SPD without any tree — that comes from the neural near field and is pre-existing.

(The far-field-free operator in §01 shows 110 negative eigenvalues, but that is an artifact of hard-truncating the far field to zero, which cannot preserve positive-definiteness. It is not an operator anyone runs.)

04

At production scale

Asymmetry grows with N and then plateaus near 1.5 %: deeper trees serve more of the far field from lumped nodes, but that fraction saturates.

05

What it costs to buy symmetry back

θ 0.3 → 0.2 cuts asymmetry 3.4× for 1.36× the step time — and cuts the far-field truncation error by the same 3.4×, since §02 showed the two move together. Going further to θ = 0.1 costs 3.1× the step time for 12×, which is a much worse trade.

06

The bigger finding: the far field carries no rotation

WarpFMM.apply slices forces[:, :3] and writes back into total_vel[:, :3]. Torques never enter the far field, and the RT, TR and RR blocks are exactly zero beyond r = 6. That is symmetric — a zero block is its own transpose — but it is a real physics truncation, and it is larger than the symmetry defect.

The damage concentrates in angular velocity, and specifically in the TR block — rotation driven by neighbours' forces, not by their torques. 07

Options

  1. Lower thetafree today

    θ = 0.2 buys 3.4× on both asymmetry and far-field accuracy for 36 % more wall time. Nothing to implement — it is a constructor argument.

  2. Symmetrized apply, u = ½(Mf + Mᵀf)

    Mᵀf needs no assembly: re-run the same traversal and scatter with atomics — for each source j inside an accepted node of target i, add M_eff[i,j]ᵀ f_i into v_j. Identical traversal means an identical pair set, so the result is symmetric by construction, exactly, at any θ.

    Cost is about 2× the far field plus atomic contention — at θ = 0.3 that is roughly +19 ms on an 89 ms step, cheaper than dropping to θ = 0.2. It is the only option giving exact symmetry without touching the vendored Warp BVH. It does not improve accuracy — the truncation error stays.

  3. Dual-tree traversal with a symmetric node–node criterion and M2L

    The principled fix: a reciprocal partition and, with matched truncation order on both sides, close to self-adjoint. A large rewrite of warp/native/bvh.{h,cu}, and worth noting that even textbook FMM with M2L is not exactly symmetric — so this buys accuracy more than it buys symmetry.

  4. Accept it

    Defensible for deterministic dynamics: 1.5 % sits inside the treecode's own truncation budget and is dwarfed by §06. Not defensible for Brownian or fluctuating hydrodynamics, where a symmetric PSD M is needed to form M¹⁄² (Cholesky or Lanczos) and to satisfy fluctuation–dissipation. This operator is neither symmetric nor PSD.

08

Found along the way

  • near_field_cutoff is a live footgun. It is a WarpFMM constructor argument, but the matching gate is hardcoded as 36.0 in warp/native/bvh.h:640. Any value other than 6.0 silently double-counts pairs (below 6) or drops them (above 6). An assert near_field_cutoff == 6.0 would close it.
  • Viscosity is ignored inside WarpFMM.apply. The far-field kernels are called with a=1.0, mu=1.0 hardcoded (treecode.py:172,181) and _gpu_near_field_pass is invoked with viscosity=1.0 (:426), so the vis_arr argument does nothing. Silent wrong answer for μ ≠ 1.
  • src/grpy.py has two bugs. tr_block = -muRT_flat.T (:124) makes the flat (6N,6N) output non-symmetric and disagrees with its own blockmatrix=True branch; and _build_cross (:136) has the opposite rotlet sign from the PyGRPY reference in src/grpy_tensors.py:14. No module imports it today, which is why it has gone unnoticed. The production paths (benchmarks/bench_rpy.py, src/grpy_tensors.py) are both correct and symmetric.
